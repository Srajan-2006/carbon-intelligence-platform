Data & Methodology

How emissions are calculated, how interventions are modeled, how the optimizer works, and — most importantly — what in this prototype is real vs. synthetic.

Data Architecture

Mine Master → Operational Activity → Emission Factors → Carbon Calculation → Interventions → Optimizer → Roadmap → KPIs

Every derived number traces back to activity data × an emission factor. Optimizer recommendations trace back to a baseline + intervention assumptions + budget + target, logged per run for audit.

Scope Definitions

Scope 1Direct emissions: diesel/fuel combustion in mine equipment, plus fugitive methane (converted to CO2e via GWP100).
Scope 2Emissions from purchased grid electricity, net of any on-site/contracted renewable share.
Scope 3Selected indirect emissions this prototype models: outbound road transport and employee commuting. Other Scope 3 categories are not yet covered.

Intervention Modeling & the Scope 1/Scope 2 Interaction

Each intervention in the library declares a fractional effect on Scope 1 and/or Scope 2 (and Scope 3 where relevant) at full deployment. Effects scale linearly with deployment level and accumulate as fractions of the mine's own baseline — they are never chained as independent multiplied percentages.

Electric haul trucks are the clearest example: converting diesel trucks to electric reduces diesel-related Scope 1 emissions, but the added electricity demand for charging increases Scope 2. The optimizer and simulator both compute Scope 1 reduction and Scope 2 increase jointly and report the net tCO2e effect — never a flat isolated "X% reduction."

How The Optimizer Works

Given a mine's baseline emissions, the intervention library, an investment budget and a target reduction percentage, the optimizer performs an exhaustive search over discrete deployment-level combinations (min → max, in fixed steps, per intervention). This is appropriate at this prototype's scale — a handful of interventions with a few levels each — and guarantees the true minimum-cost feasible package rather than a heuristic approximation. At a larger scale, this would be replaced with a formal MILP/MINLP formulation.

If a combination exists that meets the target within budget, the cheapest such combination is returned with status TARGET_MET. If none does, the optimizer reports TARGET_NOT_FEASIBLE_WITHIN_BUDGET along with the best achievable reduction and the remaining gap — it never reports success that wasn't actually found.

Limitations

  • Mine-level operational data (PM001, PM002, PM003) is synthetic prototype data, fabricated to demonstrate the architecture. It is not a measurement of any real mine.
  • Intervention costs and impact fractions are illustrative assumptions, not engineering-validated figures.
  • Scope 3 coverage is partial (transport and commuting only).
  • The optimizer uses exhaustive search appropriate to prototype scale, not production-grade mathematical programming.
  • Results are estimates under prototype assumptions — not a guarantee of real-world savings.
  • Production deployment requires replacing the synthetic operational layer with verified mine data, meters, and engineering studies, while retaining the carbon engine, intervention model, optimizer and UI.
